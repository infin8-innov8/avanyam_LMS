Training runs on spreadsheets and email

Four costs that surface the first time anyone asks what was completed, by whom, and who was qualified to teach it.

  1. No reliable record

    Completion records get assembled on demand, which is not dependable enough to stand up as evidence in an audit or an accreditation.

  2. Trainer assignment is a judgement call

    Who may teach which subject is settled informally, so nobody can say whether a qualified trainer is even available.

  3. The same details, entered again

    Qualifications, experience and skills are retyped into every system that asks for them, and the copies stop agreeing with each other.

  4. Capability you cannot see

    No consolidated view of what skills people hold, where the gaps are, or which training has quietly gone overdue.

A person holds every role that applies to them, and the active role is a session choice rather than a second account. Choose one to see what it opens.

What the platform actually does

The parts that change how the work gets done, rather than the parts that would be nice to have on a roadmap.

Assessment

Multiple choice questionnaires with an opening date, a closing date, a time limit and a pass mark.

Answers autosave as they are given, and the order differs per trainee, so two people sitting the same questionnaire are not looking at the same page.

Correct answers never reach the browser. Scoring happens on the server, and the question set freezes at the moment an attempt starts, so a later edit to the bank cannot rewrite a completed result.

Competency mapping

Administrators define the level a subject needs to be taught at.

The platform returns a ranked list of suitable trainers with its reasoning shown, and warns where a subject has no verified trainer at all.

Certification

Issuance schemes, a PDF certificate and a unique verification code.

Certificates expire per scheme wherever the scheme says they should.

Course delivery

Courses are subjects and modules, and trainers upload to them directly.

Files are malware scanned and video converted for reliable streaming before anything is released to a trainee.

Reporting that respects the role

Administrators see participation, completion, pass rates and competency gaps across the organisation.

Trainers see aggregated results for their own courses. Trainees see their own progress. Nobody reads a colleague's answers.

Getting in

Access is reviewed rather than granted on request, which is the entire reason the approval queue exists.

  1. 1

    Register

    Name, email, department, and the role you are asking for. Administrator is not on this form, by design rather than by hiding it.

  2. 2

    Trainer review

    A trainer works the queue and approves or declines, recording a reason you can read afterwards either way.

  3. 3

    Activation

    Approval activates the account and assigns the roles it was granted. Directory staff can also be created on their first single sign-on.

  4. 4

    Sign in

    Approved accounts sign in with the email and password their trainer approved. Staff accounts are sent to the corporate identity provider instead.
